🌊 Riverbend Village

It is a dry season. The well is low. Neighbors worry about water for drinking, cooking, and the farm.

You are a young community inventor. Your challenge:

Help Riverbend Village get enough clean water while keeping the community healthy, fair, and happy.

💡 Great inventors begin by noticing what people need. Look at where each family lives — the land shapes how they think about water. Listen carefully. Neighbors may disagree, and a fair fix earns Trust.

Villagers are friendly fictional characters in a learning game — not real people.

    ❓ How the Village Works

    Each simulated day, weather changes and the village uses water.

    • 🗺️ People who live in different places need different things. Read the land: Dry Hill, Riverside, Low Fields, Near the Well, and Downstream.
    • 👂 A listening meter shows how sure your forecast is. Talking to neighbors reveals their habits.
    • ⚖️ If neighbors disagree, hear both sides and choose a fair fix. Fair fixes earn Trust Points and leader powers.
    • 🏠 Homes and school need drinking water every day.
    • 🌾 The farm needs water to grow food.
    • 🌧️ Rain collectors work best on rainy days.
    • 🛢️ Tanks save water for dry days.
    • 🧪 Filters keep water clean — without them, stream water can lower health.
    • ☀️ Solar pumps move stream water, but overuse can harm the stream.
    • 💧 Drip irrigation helps the farm use less water (not for drinking).
    • 🔧 Repair benches help machines stay working.
    • 🤝 Fair sharing improves Trust. Unfair sharing lowers it.
    • 🔮 Before you run 7 days, guess what will happen — then check your guess on the report.

    Click people and places to learn. Place objects on glowing build plots. Connect a source to a tank or farm when asked. Then Run 7 Days!

    👨‍👩‍👧 Parent / Educator Notes

    Today's challenge

    Water systems and community tradeoffs — observing needs, building, testing, and improving.

    Skills practiced

    Observation · Experimentation · Systems thinking · Perspective-taking · Fair conflict resolution · Hypothesis testing · Fairness · Iteration

    Kids connect place, people, and choices: where someone lives shapes how they think about water. They hear both sides of a disagreement, try a fair fix, then guess what a 7-day test will do before they run it.

    Discussion prompts

    • How does where someone lives change what they need?
    • What made a disagreement feel fair to both sides?
    • What did you guess would happen, and why?
    • Did one part of the village do well while another had a harder week?
