🏺 Riverbend: Indus Valley

Long ago, in a land of rivers, planned brick cities, and busy docks, the rains began to weaken and a great river began to shrink.

You are a young council helper. Three cities — and small villages to the east — need a careful plan.

Help families stay safe and fed. Watch the rain and river meters, and plan ahead. When a city cannot support everyone, moving can be a wise, normal choice — not a failure. Welcome people well. Guess what the dry seasons will do, then see what happens.

💡 Look at where each city sits. The land shapes how people think about water.

This level is inspired by real Indus Valley cities, but the people, events, and numbers are imagined. It is a thinking tool, not a forecast of history.

    ❓ How the Indus Council Works

    You will guide the cities through 6 seasons as the monsoon weakens and the river shrinks.

    • 🗺️ The land shapes each city: River City trusts the big river and its wells. Dry-Land City catches rare rain in reservoirs. The Coastal Trade City looks to its dock. The East Villages have room to welcome people.
    • 🌧️ Watch the Rain and River meters. They show the dry seasons coming, so you can plan ahead.
    • 🧱 Each season you have limited bricks and workers. Choose carefully — you cannot do everything at once.
    • 👂 Talking to people fills the listening meter and makes your forecast surer.
    • 🚶 If a city cannot support everyone, you may plan a move. Moving is a careful, normal choice, not a failure.
    • ⚖️ When people disagree, hear both sides. Fair, win-win ideas earn the most Trust Points. Newcomers who are welcomed well can become helpers.
    • 🔮 Before the last dry seasons, guess whether River City's water, food, and Trust will go up, stay the same, or go down. Then check your guess.

    There is no single winning score. The report tells the story of how families fared, how fair the moves were, and what the cities left behind.

    📖 Real or imagined?

    This game mixes real history with an imagined story, so you can practice planning. Here is what is what.

    ✅ Real history (said carefully)

    • Cities like Harappa and Mohenjo-daro were carefully planned, with brick drains and many wells.
    • Dholavira built large stone reservoirs to catch rare rain.
    • Lothal had a dock-like basin. Some researchers debate exactly how it was used.
    • Indus cities traded with faraway places, including Mesopotamia.
    • The Indus script has not been deciphered, so we do not invent real inscriptions here.
    • Few clear signs of large-scale war or royal palaces have been found so far. Historians still discuss how the cities were led.
    • The cities declined over many centuries. Many researchers link this to weaker monsoons and rivers that shifted or dried, with people moving toward smaller settlements east and south.

    ✏️ Imagined in this game

    The characters, the council, the six seasons, the exact numbers, and the events you play are imagined. They are inspired by real patterns, but they are not a true story of any one year, and they are not a forecast of history or of the future.

    👨‍👩‍👧 Parent / Educator Notes

    Today's challenge

    Climate change, a shrinking river, adaptation, and migration as a dignified human response — planned with care and fair welcome.

    Skills practiced

    Systems thinking · Climate adaptation · Empathy for migrants · Fair conflict resolution · Hypothesis testing · Perspective-taking · Planning ahead

    Kids connect geography to people's needs, watch climate meters, choose tradeoffs with limited bricks and workers, plan moves when a city cannot support everyone, and test a guess before the driest seasons.

    Discussion prompts

    • How did the land of each city change what people needed?
    • Why might a family move when a river shrinks? How can a move be planned with care?
    • What made a welcome feel fair to both the hosts and the newcomers?
    • What did you guess would happen in the dry seasons, and why?
    • What did the cities leave behind that could help people later?

    Important

    This is a thinking tool, not a historical forecast and not a prediction of real-world events. Characters and events are fictional. Migration is treated with respect and dignity. There is no violence, and there are no ads, purchases, or multiplayer.
